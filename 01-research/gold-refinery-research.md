# Gold refinery  
### Overview

Gold refineries are industrial production facilities that are composed of a group of chemical engineering unit processes and unit operations refining certain materials or turning differnt metals into its purest forms by removing impurities, like turning doré (semi-pure alloys) gold and platinum into bullion.

## Site analysis

#### 1. Receiving & Preparation Area
This is where the refinery's incoming gold-bearing material first enters the facility.
Typical feed can include:  
•	Gold-bearing ore or concentrates   
•	Doré bars from mines   
•	Gold-bearing scrap or recycled material  

The material is inspected, sampled, weighed, recorded, and prepared for processing. Large or irregular material can then be sorted, crushed and ground to make it suitable for the following chemical or metallurgical stages.

#### 2. Chemical Processing Area
This is where the prepared material undergoes chemical treatment to separate gold from other substances.  
Equipment included are associated with:  
•	Leaching tanks  
•	Agitation  
•	Chemical dissolution  
•	Solution handling  
•	Solvent extraction or related separation processes  

The fundamental idea is that gold is converted into a chemical form that can be separated from unwanted metals and solids.

#### 3. Refining & Purification Area
Once gold has been separated from much of the waste material, it must be purified further.  
This area contains equipment for stages such as:  
•	Electrowinning  
•	Further chemical separation  
•	Filtration  
•	Precipitation or recovery  
•	Removal of residual impurities  

The objective is to separate gold from remaining metals such as silver, copper and other contaminants and produce a much purer gold product.

#### 4. Metal Recovery & Casting Area
After purification, the recovered gold exists as a concentrated solid or metal that must be transformed into a usable product.  
This area handles:
•	Drying and preparation of recovered material  
•	Melting  
•	Removal of remaining impurities through metallurgical treatment  
•	Casting  

Furnaces and casting equipment ultimately creates gold bars

#### 5. Utilities & Support Systems
This is the infrastructure that allows the refinery to operate continuously.  
It can include:  
•	Water supply and treatment  
•	Electrical power  
•	Heating and cooling  
•	Ventilation  
•	Waste treatment  
•	Chemical handling infrastructure  
•	Pumps and compressed utilities  
•	Environmental-control systems  

These systems don't directly produce gold, but without them the refining plant cannot safely and reliably operate.

## The Refining Process

#### Step 1 — Raw Material Input
The process begins with gold-bearing material:  
Ore / Scrap / Doré → Refinery  
The material can contain gold mixed with:  
•	Rock and mineral matter  
•	Silver  
•	Copper  
•	Iron  
•	Other metals  
•	Unwanted solids  

The refinery first identifies what has arrived and determines how it should be processed.  
The key objective at this stage is not yet to produce pure gold, but to create a controlled feed for the refinery.

#### Step 2 — Crushing & Grinding
The incoming solid material is reduced in size:  
Crusher → Conveyor → Fine particles  
Large pieces are broken down into progressively smaller particles to reduce particle size, which increases the material's accessible surface area and helps subsequent processing work more effectively.  
In ore processing, this can allow gold-bearing minerals to be liberated from surrounding rock.  
So the transformation is approximately:  
Large gold-bearing material → crushed material → fine particles  
For scrap or doré, this preparation stage can be substantially different; a refinery does not necessarily crush every kind of feed in the same way.

#### Step 3 — Leaching
The finely prepared material is brought into a leaching tank, where it is being mixed with a chemical solution capable of dissolving gold:  
Solid feed + chemical solution → gold enters solution  
Instead of remaining entirely as a solid particle, the gold becomes part of the liquid phase, while much of the unwanted solid material can remain behind.  
In some gold-extraction operations, cyanide is used for this purpose; other refinery feeds use different chemistry. The chemistry depends strongly on the feed material and desired recovery route.

#### Step 4 — Solvent Extraction & Electrowinning
This stage represents the recovery of gold from the gold-bearing solution.
##### Solvent extraction  
A chemical separation system selectively transfers certain metals between liquid phases, allowing unwanted components to be separated from the valuable metal stream.  
The goal is essentially:  
Gold-bearing solution → concentrated/cleaner gold-bearing stream  
##### Electrowinning  
The solution is then subjected to an electrical process.  
Electric current causes dissolved metal to be deposited onto an electrode, producing a concentrated metallic product or cathodic deposit/sludge.  
So the overall transformation becomes:  
Dissolved gold → electrically recovered gold-bearing solid  
This is one of the points at which the gold moves from being primarily a chemical species in a liquid to becoming a concentrated solid material.

#### Step 5 — Smelting & Melting
The recovered material is then heated in a furnace.  
An induction furnace contains molten material to:  
•	Melt the recovered metal  
•	Separate or remove remaining non-metallic material and certain impurities  
•	Combine the recovered precious-metal content into a metallic mass  
The output is then produced as impure gold/doré.  
Doré is a gold-rich alloy rather than necessarily pure gold, so it can still contain significant silver and other metals.  
The transformation is therefore:  
Recovered gold-bearing material → molten metal → doré / impure precious-metal product

#### Step 6 — Purification
The doré or intermediate gold product is subjected to another refining stage to remove the remaining impurities.  
The basic objective is:  
Doré → separate gold from remaining metals → high-purity gold  
At this stage, the gold concentration can be brought to very high levels, with the image representing approximately 99.99% purity.  
This is the stage that turns a gold-rich intermediate into a commercially useful high-purity gold product.

#### Step 7 — Final Product
Finally, the purified gold is melted and cast into a desired form:  
High-purity molten gold → molds → gold bars  
The final product can be produced as:  
•	Bars 
•	Ingots 
•	Granules 
•	Coins 
•	Other specified forms 
24-karat gold is the final product, approximately 99.99% pure gold bars.

## Precautions that are taken into consideration
### Restricted access  
Access is divided into different levels according to the person's role. The general public would not enter the processing areas; employees, contractors, visitors, and delivery personnel would only enter areas appropriate to their authorization. The perimeter is enclosed, with controlled entrances leading into the site, while sensitive areas such as refining, chemical processing, gold storage, and utilities remain within the secured operational zone.  
The layout also creates a distinction between public-facing areas—such as the entrance and administrative/visitor functions—and the industrial core, where processing equipment and valuable materials are located.  

### Operational safety  
Safety is built into the separation of activities. Heavy vehicles, pedestrians, chemical operations, furnaces, and processing equipment are kept within designated areas rather than sharing unrestricted space.  
The refinery's support systems shown in the diagram are particularly important: ventilation, fire protection, water treatment, waste management, electrical systems, and emergency infrastructure support the processing areas. Hazardous chemical handling is separated from ordinary circulation, while high-temperature operations such as smelting and casting are contained within dedicated production areas.  
The overall concept is therefore:  
controlled entry → segregated work zones → controlled material movement → secured processing → controlled product storage.  

### Security checkpoints  
The entrance is shown as a dedicated security and access-control point, separating the refinery from the surrounding road network. A real facility would typically use procedures such as identification and authorization checks, vehicle control, visitor management, monitoring, and controlled movement between internal zones.  
Rather than allowing someone to move freely through the plant, access would progressively become more restricted as the person approaches sensitive processing or storage areas.  

### Loading and receiving areas  
The receiving/loading side is positioned toward the edge of the industrial site so that trucks can enter, unload, and leave without needing to pass through the refinery's more sensitive internal areas.  
In the image, the receiving area includes space for:  
•	Incoming trucks  
•	Material unloading  
•	Stockpiles or containers  
•	Loading equipment such as forklifts and loaders  
•	Transfer toward crushing and preparation  
This creates a controlled material route:  
site entrance → receiving/loading → preparation → processing  
rather than mixing incoming deliveries with finished-product handling.  

### Separation from public spaces  
The refinery is physically separated from ordinary public activity by its site boundary, controlled entrance, internal roads, and secure perimeter. The processing buildings are set inside the property rather than directly on a public road, creating a buffer between the industrial operation and surrounding areas.  
The diagram also separates the industrial traffic network from the surrounding public road system, reducing the chance that visitors, pedestrians, or unrelated vehicles encounter heavy equipment, chemicals, or high-temperature processing.
